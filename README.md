# Lifting Sanity

**Log fast. Understand your progress. Keep your data.**

A lifting log for iPhone and iPad, made for lifters who already know how to train:

- **Fast logging.** A set done as planned is one tap; one more rep or a little more weight is three. Your split, warm-ups, left/right sets, supersets and rest timer are built in.
- **Progress you can trust.** Records, estimated 1RM (from 1–12 reps only), weekly volume and sets per muscle. Every number follows written rules (see [How the numbers are worked out](#how-the-numbers-are-worked-out)), and records are worked out again whenever your history changes.
- **Your data stays yours.** No account and no server: everything lives on your device and works without signal at the gym. Backups are a file you keep; the app reminds you to make one and keeps a few days of copies on the device as a safety net.
- **The science, when you want it.** Each exercise can show how it loads the muscle: the resistance curve, the moment arm, and a growth estimate that says what it's based on, how sure it is and what it can't know.

**Open it:** https://hoiz77.github.io/LiftingSanity-/ (after GitHub Pages is turned on, see below)

## What it does

- **Workouts that flow.** One exercise at a time with big Previous / Next buttons, swipe left or right to move, or switch to a single list. The exercise rail on the left shows how many sets are done on each.
- **A plan from the first minute.** The first time it opens, it asks how you want to start: a ready-made plan, your own split, a look around with sample workouts, or a backup from another device. Rest days pass with the calendar, and you can still train any day you like.
- **Fast set logging.** Each row shows what you did last time. Tap the check and it logs those numbers, or type new ones. Weight, reps and RIR (or RPE) for every set. On the number pad, **Next** goes from the weight to the reps to the RIR, which is a row of big 0 · 1 · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5+ keys (6 to 10 for RPE) with the target ringed: one tap fills it and moves on to the next set. **Log** logs the set straight away (with the target RIR shown, like ✓), so the RIR step only comes up when you press Next. Warm-ups skip it, and **Settings → RIR on the number pad** turns the step off.
- **Sets aren't fixed.** **Add set** adds one, **Repeat last** adds another copy of the set you just did, and **Delete** puts a red delete button on every set (tap **Done** when you're finished). Swiping a set to the left deletes it too, and every delete can be undone. Did 80 kg on the set before? The blue **↓ 80 × 6** button on the next set copies it in one tap.
- **Set types.** Working, warm-up, top set, back-off, drop set, to failure, AMRAP, rest-pause, myo-reps, cluster, partials and negatives. Each set number is drawn as a small plate in its type's colour (dashed yellow for warm-ups) and fills in when the set is done. Tap it to change the type. Warm-ups and partials stay out of your records.
- **One side at a time, your way.** Tap **Left and right** on an exercise to choose: **separate rows** (each side its own weight, reps, RIR and check), **one row** (one weight, the reps and RIR for each side, one check), or both sides together. Settings sets the default. In separate rows, one-arm and one-leg sets get a left row and a right row. The rows themselves are marked: a green **L** plate and a purple **R** plate, held together in one block with a green and purple edge. The rest timer waits until both sides are done.
- **One-side warm-ups too.** Warm-up sides keep their green or purple plate, drawn dashed with a yellow **W** tag, and read "WL" / "WR" in your history. **Warm-ups** adds one more warm-up (left and right on a one-side exercise) or a one-side warm-up on any exercise. Tap a set's number to switch just that set between one side at a time and both sides together, in a workout or in a template.
- **Exercise pictures.** 581 exercises, including 14 of the 17 machines in the Full Body EOD plan, show illustrations of the start and finish position. When a picture shows a close variation rather than the exact exercise, it says how it differs (for example "pictured with a handle; yours uses a cuff on the wrist"). Pictures appear across the top of each exercise while you train, and large in the exercise details. Every exercise also has a body map with the muscles it works in red. Pictures for your split are saved on the device for the gym. You can add your own photo of any machine with your camera; photos stay on the device and are included in backups.
- **Rest timer.** Starts by itself when you check a set, with −15 / +15 / Skip and a sound when it's over. Each exercise remembers its own rest.
- **Supersets.** Link two exercises and it moves you back and forth, resting only after the round.
- **18 ready-made plans, from 2 to 6 days a week.** Full Body, Full Body ×2, Upper / Lower / Full, Upper / Lower, Torso / Limbs, Anterior / Posterior, Push / Pull, PHUL, 5×5, Upper / Lower + PPL, PHAT, Bro Split, Push / Pull / Legs, Upper / Lower ×6, Anterior / Posterior ×6, Arnold Split, Dumbbells Only and Full Body EOD. Narrow the list by days a week and experience (it starts from the guide's answers), and open any plan to see who it's for, a week laid out, every day's exercises, how long a session takes and how it's built, with the studies behind it, before you use it. Build and edit your own templates and splits too.
- **Built on the evidence.** The plans aim for 10 to 20 hard sets a week for the main muscles (fewer in the plans for beginners), each muscle on two or more days a week (with the same weekly sets that hardly changes growth, but it keeps sessions shorter and helps strength a little; the Bro Split says it trains each muscle once), working sets 1 to 2 reps short of failure, 2 to 3 minutes' rest on big lifts and 60 to 90 seconds on small ones, sessions of about an hour (a rule of thumb), push and pull in balance, and exercises with direct comparisons behind them: overhead triceps extensions, seated leg curls, standing calf raises, preacher curls (incline curls didn't grow the biceps in the one trial that tested them) and leg extensions besides squats for the quad that crosses the hip. Cable and dumbbell lateral raises grew the side delts the same, so either works. A test runs the plan review on every plan and version and fails if any of that slips.
- **Machines first for beginners.** Every gym plan has a machine version: barbell, dumbbell and pull-up lifts become machines and cables that do the same job (bench press → chest press machine, squat → leg press, pull-up → lat pulldown…), while sets, reps and rest stay the same. Muscle grows about as well on either. The guide gives it to anyone just starting, and **Move to free weights** on your split swaps everything back in one tap when you're ready (and back again).
- **1,656 exercises.** Barbell, dumbbell, cable, machine, bodyweight, kettlebell, bands, strongman, Olympic lifts, cardio and mobility, plus step-by-step **How to do it** instructions for over 1,300 of them. Add your own.
- **New records you can feel.** Beat a record mid-workout and a gold **New PR!** banner drops in with confetti, a stronger tap and (with sound on) a short fanfare. It shows what you beat and by how much ("Heaviest 85 kg +2.5 kg"), the row glows, and a trophy count in the header keeps score.
- **An ending worth finishing for.** Finish opens a full-screen celebration: a medal, confetti and "Great work!" (or "New records!", "Workout #100!", "First workout done!"). Then come cards to swipe, each one ready to share as a 1080 × 1350 picture: the workout (weight lifted counting up, what that's as heavy as, time, sets, reps and an anatomical muscle map that glows where you worked), your records with what they beat, the exercises with their pictures, sets and best set, the muscles worked front and back, "Getting stronger" charts of each lift's best set over the last sessions, your week streak, against last time (weight lifted, exercises improved, biggest jump) and milestones (workout counts and lifetime weight). Below: every record, sets per muscle, the note and template buttons. Any workout in History has **Share** too.
- **Your colours.** Settings → Colours: every colour in the app. The main ones (accent, done, records, delete and warnings), the background, cards, text and secondary text for dark mode and for light mode separately (the lines and greys follow the cards), the left and right sides, and each set type. Pick from swatches, a colour wheel with any colour, or a hex code, or take a ready-made look: X Dim (grey-blue, like X's Dim), Lights out, Graphite, Midnight, Ocean, Crimson, Violet, Forest, Sunset, Paper or Mono. A live preview made of real workout pieces (header, sets, set-type plates, switches, Delete, the number pad) shows the change in dark and light before you save. It stays readable on its own: light colours get dark text on them, a dark-mode background is kept dark and a light one light, text too faint for its cards is brightened until it reads, and in light mode each colour is darkened just enough; a note says when a colour is shown a little differently. The end screen and its share cards use your colours too. **Y2K has its own set**: its frame, accent and highlight neons, background, text and the rest, with looks like Vaporwave, Matrix, Red alert, Amber, Ice, Sakura and Chrome; each style keeps its own colours.
- **Progress.** Your week as a `weekly_stats.log` card you can share, estimated 1RM, heaviest weight and volume charts per exercise, best weight for every rep count, weekly volume, sets per muscle, records and a week streak. Every session is numbered.
- **Smart suggestions.** Hit the top of your rep range on every set and it suggests adding weight next time. One tap adds warm-up sets, and a plate calculator shows what to load on each side.
- **Reset progression, keep your history.** Start a new block (new program, after a break or an injury) and your records and charts start fresh. Earlier blocks stay saved and viewable, and the All time view still shows everything.
- **Your machine's resistance profile.** Cams differ from one machine to the next, so for machines you can say where yours feels hardest: even, at the start, in the middle, at the end, or draw your own curve by dragging five points. It's in the exercise's "How it works" and in its ⋯ menu during a workout. The curve, the diagram's animation and the growth rating follow your choice (hardest in the stretch scores higher, light in the stretch lower), and so do Best for growth and the plan review.
- **Movement animations.** Exercise pictures fade from start to finish at rep tempo (lift about 1 second, squeeze, lower about 2 seconds, pause in the stretch). The "How it works" diagram moves through the same rep, with the line of force and the moment arm changing as it goes and a dot running along the resistance curve. Only what is on screen moves. Turn them off in Settings → Movement animations; they also stay still when Reduce Motion is on.
- **Reset one exercise.** From the exercise's ⋯ menu in a workout, the ↺ next to it under Progress → Your lifts, or its details. Only that exercise starts fresh (records, charts, weight tips); its card shows "Fresh since …", and every reset can be undone.
- **Notes that are easy to reach.** Tap **Note** on an exercise to write today's note right on the card, with quick taps like "Felt strong" or "Pain / discomfort". Last time's note shows too, and notes appear in each exercise's history. At the end, the finish screen's "How did it go?" box has words to tap in: "Great session", "Tired", "Slept badly", "Good pump"…
- **Machine setup, one tap away.** Tap **Setup** on an exercise and tap the parts that apply (seat, back pad, handles, range lock… or pulley height and attachment for cables, bench angle and rack height for barbells), typing each one's setting. It stays on the card as its own line every time you do that exercise.
- **Moves along with you.** Finish the last set of an exercise and the app goes to the next unfinished one: it slides in when you see one exercise at a time, and scrolls down to it (with a short glow) in the list. Scroll anywhere you like; the exercise you're on is remembered, so closing and reopening the app brings you back to it.
- **How it works, for every exercise.** Joint actions, the muscles and heads involved, a diagram of the setup with the line of force and the moment arm (the cuff on your cuffed pushdown included), the resistance profile (ascending, descending, bell-shaped or even) worked out from the geometry, and how well it loads the muscle in the stretch. Each exercise gets a growth estimate out of 10 (labelled as an estimate, with the points behind it and what it can't see), with what studies found where a direct comparison exists, tips, and higher-rated alternatives. The Exercises tab can sort by **Best for growth**.
- **Review my plan.** The **Review** button on your split checks it against the training literature: weekly sets per muscle (helper muscles count half), how often each muscle is trained, effort (reps in reserve, planned failure on heavy lifts, and how far off logged RIR tends to be), exercise choice (overhead vs pushdown triceps work, seated vs lying leg curls, standing vs seated calf raises, leg extensions besides squats, lengthened partials), rest times on big and small lifts, session length (with supersets that don't cost reps) and push/pull balance, with links to the studies.
- **What the research says.** **Settings → Guide** has short answers, each with its studies: taking a break, protein, training over 65, women, deloads, rep speed, machines or free weights, and training to failure.
- **Smooth from screen to screen.**
  - The workout grows up out of its bar at the bottom when you open it, and shrinks back into the bar when you minimize it.
  - Switching tabs fades and slides the page in from the side of its tab, and the tab highlight glides across.
  - Sheets slide up and down. Pull one down by its top bar, or from anywhere once its content is scrolled to the top, to close it. A sheet opened over another one makes the one below step back.
  - In one-at-a-time mode the exercise card follows your finger: let go past a quarter of the way, or flick, for the next or previous one.
  - The number pad and rest timer slide in and out. Pictures on screen stay put when a page redraws, so nothing flashes white.
  - With Reduce Motion on, screens fade instead of moving.
- **A guide for new people.** On a new phone, **Set me up** asks five quick questions (how long you've been lifting, your goal, days a week, gym or home, how you like to learn), then recommends a plan with why it fits (its machine version if you're just starting at a gym), a couple of others you can open and compare, and settings to match (rest for the goal; effort tracking off while you learn). Then your choice: a six-part tour, a short tip the first time you open each part, or nothing. **Settings → Guide** opens it all again, with the words you'll see (RIR, 1RM, working set, superset, block…) explained.
- **English or Arabic.** Settings → Language (and on the welcome). In Arabic the whole app reads right to left: the tab rail and the workout list sit on the right, arrows point the other way, and swiping to the next exercise goes the way you read. Numbers stay 0–9, the number pad keeps 1 2 3 left to right, and charts run left to right as in most apps. Everything else is in Arabic, the exercise-science text included. Two more choices, both off to start: exercise names in Arabic (search still finds them by either name, and an exercise's page keeps its English name), and units and gym terms in Arabic (كغ for kg, احتياط for RIR, أقصى رفعة for 1RM, Arabic set letters). How-to steps stay in English. A phone set to Arabic starts in Arabic the first time.
- **Two styles.** Settings → Style: **Clean** (default) or **Y2K**: cobalt and chrome, pixel and LCD type, bevelled windows, an LCD rest timer, EQ-style bars and a live HUD.
- **What's new.** After an update, a short note says what changed (once, never over an open workout, and one tap puts it away). Settings → What's new shows it again with the updates before.

## How the numbers are worked out

These rules are used everywhere the app counts or compares sets (workout header, exercise strip, finish screen, records, charts, weight tips and the plan review). Run `LiftingSanity.selfCheck()` in the browser console to check them against the app's own code; it uses made-up sets and never touches your saved workouts.

- **Working sets.** Warm-ups never count. A left + right pair is one set, not two, and it's done once both sides are, everywhere: the header, the exercise strip, the Train card and the finish prompt. A pair with only one side checked is left out when you finish (the finish prompt tells you, so you can check the other side first). Each row of a drop set, rest-pause or myo-rep set counts as its own set (a rule of thumb: studies compared whole drop-set series with normal sets, not row by row). For weekly volume, helper muscles get half a set.
- **Checking a set logs what's shown.** The grey suggestions in a set (weight, reps and the target RIR) become the logged values when you tap ✓ (or **Log** on the pad's RIR step), unless you typed or picked something else. If the set can't be checked yet (no reps), nothing in it changes.
- **Suggestions follow the set's type.** A set suggests what you did last time in the same set. Later sets of the same type follow a weight you changed today; a warm-up follows last time's warm-up (warm-ups ramp up), a back-off set never takes the top set's weight, a drop set comes down about 20% from the set just before it (a rule of thumb), and the second side of a left/right set uses the first side's weight. The "add weight" tip goes to working sets, not warm-ups, back-offs, drops, partials or negatives. A target like "120s" is 120 seconds.
- **Volume.** Weight × reps for every checked working set; for left/right sets, each side's weight × reps (both limbs did the work).
- **Estimated 1RM.** Epley (weight × (1 + reps / 30)), and only from sets of 1 to 12 reps with a weight above zero. Higher-rep sets still count for volume, heaviest weight and most reps; they just don't feed the 1RM estimate, which gets unreliable past about 12 reps. Nothing is deleted or rewritten.
- **Records.** Warm-ups, partials and eccentric-only sets are never records. Est. 1RM and heaviest-weight records follow the rule above. An exercise done twice in one workout gets one record of each kind (its best). Records are worked out again across your history whenever it changes: editing, deleting or back-logging a workout, removing samples, or undoing a reset. A reset made during a workout counts that workout as after the reset.
- **Weight tips.** "Try X today" only comes from sessions since the exercise's last reset (or the current block), never from before it.
- **Left/right on and off.** Switching never invents or doubles a set. Sets you already checked stay exactly as you did them. A planned left/right pair becomes one set using the first side's numbers (never mixing the two sides), and switching back gives the same two sides again. In one-row left/right, if the two sides were logged with different weights, both are shown and checking the set doesn't overwrite either.
- **kg and lb.** Weights are stored in kg and converted only for display, so switching units back and forth never changes what's stored.
- **Saving.** Changes are saved within a second, and straight away when the app goes to the background or is closed. If the device can't save (storage full), you're told. Saved data with something odd in it (a workout missing its exercises, a day whose template was deleted) is mended on opening instead of stopping the app. If saved data can't be read at all, it's copied aside first; if there's no room for the copy, the app never writes over it.
- **Backups.** A backup holds everything: workouts, templates, splits, settings and style, exercise setups and notes, machine profiles, resets and your photos. Restoring checks the whole file first; a damaged backup changes nothing. A restore replaces everything, photos included (photos are swapped only once the data is in). Erase everything also removes the copy kept from before a restore.
- **Growth estimates.** Each exercise's score out of 10 is an estimate from its mechanics, not a measured result: points for loading the stretched position (up to 3, and less where a direct trial found no extra growth from the stretch: lateral raises and incline curls), stability (2), tension through the range (2), hitting the target with little extra fatigue (2) and being easy to add load to (1). The details show the points, label the score "Estimate", and say what it can't see (your joints, technique, how close to failure you go, your program). Where studies looked at that kind of exercise, they're shown and count for more than the score. Stretches, conditioning drills and anything done for time or distance get no score, and neither does anything the model doesn't cover; they say so instead of showing a guess. Bands are scored as light in the stretch, because they get heavier as they stretch. In the plan review, each point is marked as coming from studies (with links) or as a rule of thumb.
- **Backups and copies.** Settings shows when you last backed up. After a few workouts with no backup, or a week and new workouts since the last one, the Train page reminds you ("Later" waits 3 days). The app also keeps one copy of your data a day on the device, for the last 5 days, that you can go back to; they're on the device only, so they don't replace a backup file.

## Turn on GitHub Pages (once)

1. Open https://github.com/Hoiz77/LiftingSanity-/settings/pages (this is **Settings → Pages** in the repository).
2. Under **Build and deployment**, set **Source** to **Deploy from a branch**.
3. Pick the **main** branch and the **/ (root)** folder, then **Save**.
4. After a minute the site is live at https://hoiz77.github.io/LiftingSanity-/

## Put it on your phone

The same link works on iPhone, iPad and Android. The layout adapts to the screen, and on phones you type weights and reps on a built-in number pad with Next, + / − and Log keys (and pick the RIR on it with one tap), so the keyboard never covers your sets.

1. Open the link above: in **Safari** on iPhone or iPad, in **Chrome** on Android.
2. iPhone / iPad: tap **Share**, then **Add to Home Screen**. Android: tap **⋮**, then **Add to Home screen** or **Install app**.
3. Open Lifting Sanity from your Home Screen.

## Share it with friends

Send them the link. Each person gets their own copy of the app with an empty log and a welcome that asks a few questions and finds them a plan that fits; nobody sees anyone else's workouts, because nothing leaves the phone it was logged on. There's no account to make. Tell them to back up now and then from **Settings → Back up**: with no account, that file is the only copy off their phone.

Your workouts are saved on each device itself, not online, so the iPad and the iPhone each keep their own log. Make a backup now and then from **Settings → Back up** and keep the file in Files or iCloud Drive. **Restore** loads it on any device, which is also how you move your log from one to the other.

## Files

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `index.html` | The page and all of its styling |
| `app.js` | Everything the app does |
| `exercises.js` | The exercise library and the ready-made splits |
| `visuals.js` | Which illustration goes with each exercise, and whether it is exact or similar |
| `art/ek/` | Everkinetic line drawings (CC BY-SA 4.0, see its README) |
| `science.js` | Movement patterns, the mechanics model, ratings and the studies |
| `y2k.css` | The optional Y2K style |
| `library-extra.js` | About 1,200 more exercises, made from [exercises-dataset](https://github.com/hasaneyldrm/exercises-dataset) |
| `steps.json` | Step-by-step how-to instructions, loaded the first time you open an exercise's details |
| `sw.js` | Keeps the app on the device so it opens offline |
| `manifest.webmanifest`, `icon-*.png` | Home Screen name and icon |
| `i18n.js`, `ar.js`, `ar-names.js` | Languages: picks English or Arabic before the page draws, the Arabic text, and Arabic exercise names |
| `tests/` | Automated tests that use the app in a phone-sized browser; see [tests/README.md](tests/README.md) |

No build step and no dependencies for the app. Edit a file, push, and the site updates. To run the tests: `npm install`, `npx playwright install chromium`, then `npm test`. They also run on every push (GitHub Actions).
